# Content workflow

Use the lightest process that protects the idea. A single short video can move through these stages in one document; a research-heavy essay benefits from linked notes and a source log. Every project should have a visible status and a next action.

## Suggested project folder

```text
content/drafts/YYYY-MM-short-working-title/
  idea.md
  research.md
  sources.md
  outline.md
  draft.md
  visual-brief.md
  assets/
```

Create only the files the project needs. Keep final website implementation in the existing `notes/` and `scripts/` locations and use the existing listing pages (`notes.html`, `work.html`) when a piece is ready to be shown.

## Stages and gates

### 1. Idea — capture the question

Start with `content-idea-template.md`. Name the audience, the question or tension, why it matters now, and the possible original angle. Record a few formats and the next small step. Park ideas that do not yet have a clear audience or useful question.

**Move on when:** the idea can be stated as a question or tension, and there is a plausible reason this audience would care.

### 2. Research — understand before asserting

Use `research-notes-template.md` to map what is known, what is uncertain, and what different explanations compete. Record interpretations separately from findings. Use `source-log-template.md` for sources, exact claims, limitations, and follow-up checks. Seek primary evidence where practical and check whether a source supports the wording you plan to use.

**Move on when:** the central claims have traceable support, material uncertainty is visible, and the piece has an original observation or synthesis beyond summarizing sources.

### 3. Outline — make the logic visible

Use `outline-template.md` to arrange the thesis, stakes, evidence, examples, counterargument, and conclusion. For video, note the hook, narrative turns, transitions, and visual opportunities. Remove sections that only repeat the thesis.

**Move on when:** each section has a job, the conclusion follows from the argument, and the audience can understand why each transition happens.

### 4. Draft — write for the format

Choose `article-template.md`, `long-form-script-template.md`, or `short-form-script-template.md`. Write for the reader or viewer, not for the template: replace prompts, remove unused sections, and keep spoken language natural when writing a script. Add citations or source markers while drafting so they are not lost.

**Move on when:** the full piece has a clear through-line, concrete examples, and a payoff that answers the opening question.

### 5. Review — strengthen truth and clarity

Review the piece once for argument and once for expression. Check the thesis, audience fit, examples, counterarguments, claim strength, attribution, and ending. Verify names, dates, quotations, numbers, and causal language against the source log. Ask what would change your mind and whether a reasonable alternative explanation is represented fairly.

For scripts, read aloud and time the result. For articles, test whether headings and paragraphs make the argument easy to follow. Do not state confidence or persuasion as guaranteed psychological effects; distinguish evidence from interpretation.

**Move on when:** factual claims are supported or explicitly qualified, attribution is accurate, and no important disagreement is hidden.

### 6. Packaging — prepare how it will appear

Use `thumbnail-brief-template.md` for artwork. Select the final title and write a useful excerpt or description. Confirm image orientation, aspect ratio, crop-safe area, alt text, and the intended asset path. Choose appropriate internal links and the correct site destination.

For this static site, essays belong in the Notes pattern (`notes/` and `notes.html`); scripts and visual concepts follow the Selected Work pattern (`scripts/` and `work.html`). Preserve its editorial direction: near-black, warm ivory, restrained red, strong typography, clean spacing, and minimal effects.

**Move on when:** title, metadata, visual assets, internal links, destination, and listing copy are ready and consistent.

### 7. Publish — check the actual page

Complete `publishing-checklist.md`. Check local links and image paths, page title and description, social image metadata, alt text, keyboard access, mobile layout, and the listing-page entry. Confirm that the content is readable without relying on an external asset or account. Record the publication URL, date, channel, and final status.

**Move on when:** the published page matches the approved draft and all required checks are complete. Publication or deployment remains a separate deliberate release action.

### 8. Learn — close the loop

Record reader or viewer questions, corrections, useful feedback, and follow-on ideas. Do not treat reach or engagement as evidence that a factual claim is true. Add promising unanswered questions to the idea template and preserve correction notes with the project.

## Lightweight review pass

- Can the central idea be said in one sentence?
- What is original here: an observation, example, synthesis, or application?
- Which claims are facts, interpretations, or hypotheses?
- What is the strongest fair alternative explanation?
- Does every section move the reader or viewer forward?
- Is the packaging accurate to the work itself?
- Can someone use the published page comfortably on a phone?
